# How Often Should You Replace Your Robot Vacuum Filter?

## Short answer

- **Most homes**: every 60–90 days.
- **Homes with pets**: every 30–45 days.
- **Homes with multiple long-haired pets**: every 30 days, no exceptions.

This is the answer for HEPA filters across the major robot vacuum brands. Brand-specific manuals will tell you the same range.

## Three signs you waited too long

### 1. The filter is gray, not white

Tap it on the trash can. If it doesn't go back to off-white, the fibers are saturated. New filter.

### 2. Suction has dropped (you can hear it)

A clogged filter chokes the airflow. The motor pitch drops about a half-step. Once you've heard the difference once, you'll hear it every time.

### 3. The exhaust smells

The dust cake on a saturated filter starts to hold odors — pet smell, cooking smell, musk. If the air leaving the robot has a smell, the filter is past its life.

## Filter, brush, mop pad — what's on the same schedule and what isn't

| Part | Replacement cadence | Lifespan |
|---|---|---|
| HEPA filter | 60–90 days (30–45 with pets) | 3–6 months |
| Main brush (silicone V-shape) | 6 months | 9–12 months |
| Side brush | 3–6 months | 6 months |
| Mop pads | 2–3 months | depends on use |
| Battery | 18–24 months | 2–3 years |

Filters are the only consumable on a strict schedule. Brushes and mop pads degrade visibly — you'll see them go before they fail.
