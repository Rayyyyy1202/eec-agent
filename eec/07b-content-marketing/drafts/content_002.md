Real lidar — the spinning laser-on-top-of-the-puck kind — used to be a $799 floor. In 2026, three robots clear $500. Here's what each one actually delivers, and what you give up at this price.

## Why $500 is the new lidar threshold

Two things changed: solid-state lidar modules dropped to under $20 in BOM, and SLAM software stopped requiring a separate chip. The result: a 2024-era $800 robot's mapping experience now ships at $449.

Below $500 you're not getting a budget robot anymore. You're getting last year's flagship without the auto-wash dock.

## Lidar vs camera vs gyro: a 60-second primer

- **Gyro** (cheapest): an internal compass. Drifts. Re-runs the same line three times because it forgot. Expect random patterns.
- **Camera (vSLAM)** (mid): a downward camera reads floor texture. Hates dark floors. Hates the dark.
- **Lidar (true SLAM)** (best): spinning laser builds a 1cm-resolution map. Works in pitch dark. Doesn't drift. Recognizes furniture. Routes optimally.

If you have hardwood, low light, or want consistent coverage week to week — lidar is the only category that delivers.

## Buying checklist

Before you click buy on any sub-$500 lidar robot:

- [ ] Confirm it's a real spinning lidar puck, not a "lidar-assist" hybrid (read the spec sheet, not the headline).
- [ ] Check the brush type — V-shape silicone wins for pet households.
- [ ] Verify the warranty length. 1 year is the floor; 2 years should be standard at $400+.
- [ ] Find out the cost of replacement filters and brushes. A robot that needs $89 of consumables every 3 months is not actually $449.
